# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This project exists to be useful to people building with AI agents, whatever their
background, experience level, or first language. Everyone participating — issues, pull
requests, discussions, reviews — is expected to help keep it a place where that is
possible.

## Expected behavior

- Be direct about technical disagreement and kind about people. Criticize the design, the
  code, or the claim — not the person who wrote it.
- Assume competence and good faith. Ask what someone meant before concluding they were
  wrong.
- Accept that "no" is a legitimate answer, and that maintainers may decline a change
  without it being a judgment on the contributor.
- Remember that many contributors are not writing in their first language. Read for intent
  and answer plainly rather than correcting form.
- Give credit and cite sources. When you build on someone else's work or report, say so.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Personal, demeaning, or discriminatory remarks — including about nationality, ethnicity,
  gender, sexual orientation, religion, disability, age, or level of experience.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately submitting insecure or malicious code, or misrepresenting what a change
  does.
- Sustained bad-faith argument after a decision has been made and explained.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions —
and whenever someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness behavior that violates this Code of Conduct, report it
privately to **vuongdam2k01@gmail.com**. Reports are handled confidentially. Include what
happened, where, and links if they exist.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this Code of Conduct in good faith are subject to
the same consequences as anyone else.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will respond in a way proportionate to the behavior. Depending on severity and
whether it repeats, that may mean:

1. A private correction explaining what was wrong.
2. A public warning.
3. A temporary ban from interaction with the project.
4. A permanent ban.

Deliberate malicious code submissions skip directly to a permanent ban.
